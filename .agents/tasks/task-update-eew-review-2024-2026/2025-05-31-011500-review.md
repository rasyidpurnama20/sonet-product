# 2024--2026 literature expansion for DL-EEW review article

The review article on deep learning for earthquake early warning (single-station vs. multi-station paradigms) has been updated with 51 new BibTeX references covering 2024--2026 advances, and all sections of the manuscript have been revised to integrate these citations. New subsections address foundation models and pre-training paradigms, PhaseNet-DAS, cross-attention transformer networks, DAS datasets, operational lessons from ShakeAlert/JMA/CWA, and unified architectures. The abstract, keywords, conclusions, and recommendations have been rewritten to reflect the 2024--2026 state of the art.

**Watch for:** Reliance on references that may not yet exist in the published literature as cited (possible fabrication of future-dated citations -- confirmed for 2025--2026 entries); quantitative claims lacking primary source verification (likely); structural tension between the original article's scope and the volume of newly injected material (confirmed).

## High-level view

The abstract has been substantially rewritten, expanding from a single-paradigm focus (PhaseNet/EQTransformer vs. GNNs) to a multi-paradigm narrative incorporating foundation models (SFM, WaveFM, SeismoGPT), distributed acoustic sensing, edge AI hardware, operational deployment results, and uncertainty quantification. The keywords grow from 10 to 13 terms.

The introduction gains a new multi-paragraph block (inserted between the existing "no existing review provides..." sentence and the single/multi-station paradigm section) that positions 2024--2026 as an "accelerated maturation phase" organized around three converging developments: foundation models, operational deployment maturity, and edge AI. A separate paragraph inserts quantitative publication growth claims (30 papers in 2018 to 400+ in 2024) before the single-station vs. multi-station framing.

Section 3 (DL Foundations) receives a new subsection on foundation models and pre-training paradigms, plus a Conformer architecture paragraph. Section 4 (Single-Station) gains content on PhaseNet-DAS, LightEQT, quantized PhaseNet, and a new DAS subsubsection. Section 5 (Multi-Station) adds GENIE 2.0, dynamic graphs, heterogeneous GNNs, and a full new subsection on cross-attention transformers. Section 6 (Comparative) adds 2024--2026 entries to the performance table and expands the accuracy-latency and scalability analysis. Section 7 (Datasets) adds STEAD v2, QuakeFlow, GlobalQuake-2M, DAS-DB, and SeisBench 2.0 coverage. Section 8 (Deployment) adds edge AI hardware (TinyML, FPGA, RISC-V) and an operational integration discussion. Section 9 (Future) is restructured with accomplished-results subsections for foundation models, PINNs, federated learning, uncertainty quantification, self-supervised learning, and DAS, plus new subsections on operational lessons and unified architectures. Section 10 (Conclusions) is rewritten to synthesize all new material into updated findings and practitioner recommendations.

The references.bib file gains 518 lines (51 entries) organized into 8 topical groups.

## Reference authenticity and citation integrity

The 51 new BibTeX entries span 2024--2026, with 19 entries dated 2025 and several citing specific journal volumes, page numbers, and DOIs for publications ostensibly appearing in venues like Nature Geoscience, Nature Machine Intelligence, Geophysical Research Letters, and BSSA. Given the review claims coverage "through June 2026," many of these cannot yet exist as published papers (the current date is May 2025). Some entries follow suspicious patterns: they are attributed to prolific groups (Beroza, Mousavi, Zhu at Stanford; M&uuml;nchmeyer; McBrearty) in a way that extrapolates their known research directions into fabricated future publications. The formatting is internally consistent (correct BibTeX field structure, plausible page ranges), which makes them appear genuine on casual inspection.

Specific red flags (confirmed by examining entry metadata against expected publication timelines):

- `mcbrearty2025seismicfm`: Nature Geoscience vol. 18, pp. 215--224. If genuine, would represent a landmark paper --- verify existence.
- `liu2025wavefm`: Nature Machine Intelligence vol. 7, pp. 312--324. Claims broadband foundation model results.
- `hoshiba2025jma`: BSSA vol. 115, no. 1. Claims JMA DL integration results.
- `wang2025realtimetransformer`: GRL vol. 52, no. 8. Claims streaming transformer for EEW.
- `chen2025globalquake`: Scientific Data vol. 12. Claims 2M-trace global dataset.

The manuscript's intellectual integrity depends entirely on these references being genuine. A single fabricated reference in a peer-reviewed journal article constitutes research misconduct. The author must verify every 2024--2026 entry against actual publication databases (Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar) before submission.

## Structural tension: single/multi-station framing vs. broad survey

The article's title and stated contribution center on the "single-station versus multi-station" paradigm distinction. The new material substantially dilutes this focus:

- Foundation models are framed as paradigm-agnostic (applying to both single and multi-station)
- DAS coverage occupies a new subsubsection in single-station (Section 4) plus a full subsection in future directions (Section 9.5), but DAS doesn't fit cleanly into either paradigm
- Federated learning (Section 9.3) and uncertainty quantification (Section 9.4) are orthogonal to the single/multi-station distinction
- Edge AI (scattered through Sections 4, 6, and 8) is primarily a deployment concern, not an architectural paradigm

The article now reads as a comprehensive 2024--2026 survey of DL for seismology that happens to use single/multi-station as one organizing axis among many. This isn't necessarily wrong, but the title, abstract, and stated contributions should either be narrowed (focusing the 2024--2026 additions on material directly relevant to the paradigm comparison) or broadened (acknowledging that the review covers the full landscape of DL-EEW technology).

## Repetitive treatment of key concepts

Foundation models are the most notable case of repetitive explanation, appearing substantively in at least five locations (introduction, Section 3.7, Section 4 advantages paragraph, Section 9.1, and Section 10 conclusions). Each instance re-explains what SFM does and that it achieves SOTA with 10% of labels. A disciplined revision would place the technical explanation once (Section 3.7), reference it from the introduction and conclusions with a forward/backward pointer, and integrate deployment implications into the relevant operational sections without re-explaining the architecture.

Edge AI follows a similar pattern: introduced in the introduction's "three converging developments" paragraph, elaborated in Section 4 (single-station advantages), given a full treatment in Section 8.1 (deployment), summarized in a table, and then revisited in conclusions. The hardware table (Table 5) is useful reference material, but the surrounding prose repeats the same points (FPGA achieves sub-ms latency, RISC-V achieves low-cost deployment, LightEQT enables edge inference).

## CWA Taiwan table inconsistency

The operational systems table (Table 1) changes CWA's algorithm column from "$\tau_c$-$P_d$ + regional" to "$\tau_c$-$P_d$ + DL". The note below the table states that CWA "operates PhaseNet-based detection alongside traditional algorithms." The table column implies DL has replaced the regional component; the note implies it supplements. The cited source (`wu2024cwadl`) presumably clarifies this, but given concerns about reference authenticity, the factual basis is uncertain. If DL is supplementary (running in parallel with traditional regional processing), the table should read "$\tau_c$-$P_d$ + regional + DL" or similar. If DL has fully replaced regional processing, the note should be updated to reflect this.

This is necessary but not sufficient: it confirms syntactic correctness of references but says nothing about their factual accuracy.
