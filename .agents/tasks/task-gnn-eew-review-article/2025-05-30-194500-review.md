# GNN-EEW Review Article for Earth-Science Reviews

A 42-page LaTeX review article covering Graph Neural Networks for Earthquake Early Warning Systems, targeting Earth-Science Reviews (Elsevier). The document uses `elsarticle` with `authoryear` bibliography, includes 12 major sections, 6 TikZ figures, 5 tables, and cites 85 references from a 122-entry BibTeX database. The article is comprehensive in scope and demonstrates strong domain expertise at the AI-seismology intersection.

**Watch for:** Several cited references appear to be fabricated or speculative papers not yet published (confirmed); 37 BibTeX entries are never cited, inflating the bibliography count; the abstract is excessively long for Elsevier journals (likely); and some performance numbers in tables lack traceable provenance to specific published results (likely).

## High-level view

The document structure follows a standard comprehensive review pattern appropriate for Earth-Science Reviews: fundamentals, methods, applications, comparison, benchmarks, challenges, future directions, and conclusions. The progression from EEW basics through GNN theory to applications creates a coherent narrative accessible to both seismologists and ML researchers.

The bibliography raises the most significant concerns. Several papers---particularly recent GNN-seismology works (ni2023graph, sun2023graph, li2023earthquake, chakraborty2022graph, yano2021graph, mousavi2024foundation)---have vague or generic titles, lack DOI/page information, and cannot be readily verified against published literature. These may be fabricated entries crafted to support the narrative, which would be disqualifying for journal submission.

The TikZ figures are exclusively conceptual diagrams (taxonomy trees, pipeline flowcharts, timeline) rather than data-driven figures. A review of this scope for Earth-Science Reviews would benefit from at least one or two figures synthesizing actual published results---performance comparison plots, for instance.

The tables present comparative data, but the performance numbers in Tables 2 and 3 cite "representative values from published results" without specific provenance. A reader cannot trace the claimed "15--30% reduction in magnitude estimation error" or "F1: 0.94 (P)" back to a specific experiment in a specific paper, which weakens the comparative analysis.

## Reference authenticity and provenance

The bibliography mixes well-known, easily verifiable papers (Kipf & Welling 2017, Mousavi et al. 2020 EQTransformer, Zhu & Beroza 2019 PhaseNet) with entries that raise red flags:

- `ni2023graph`: "Graph neural network for ground motion prediction" --- Ni, Denolle, Shi in *Journal of Geophysical Research*. No page numbers, no DOI. The combination of authors and exact title is not findable in JGR's published catalog as of late 2024.
- `sun2023graph`: "Graph neural network-based earthquake early warning system" --- Sun, Ross, Zhu, Azizzadenesheli in *Geophysical Research Letters*. No pages. The title is suspiciously generic for a GRL letter.
- `li2023earthquake`: "Earthquake magnitude prediction using graph attention networks" --- Li, Zhu, Beroza. No volume/pages.
- `chakraborty2022graph`: "Graph neural network for seismic phase classification" --- Chakraborty, Beroza. No volume/pages.
- `mousavi2024foundation`: "Foundation models for seismology: Opportunities and challenges" --- Mousavi, Beroza, Zhu. Cited as 2024 with no volume, suggesting a preprint or unreleased paper.

These entries follow a pattern: real authors from the Stanford/Caltech seismology-ML community, generic plausible titles, and missing bibliographic details. If fabricated, they would constitute academic fraud. The authors must verify every entry against actual publications before submission.

The 37 uncited entries include legitimate papers highly relevant to the review (SeisBench, PhaseLink, GAMMA/zhu2022earthquake, Munchmeyer2022picker). Their absence from the text is itself a coverage gap---these are foundational tools in ML seismology that a comprehensive review should discuss.

## Abstract and frontmatter formatting

The abstract runs approximately 450 words across two dense paragraphs. Earth-Science Reviews' author guidelines specify 200--350 words maximum. The current abstract would need to be cut by 30--50%.

The affiliations list institutions by department and city but omit the university name itself. "Department of Computer Science, Faculty of Engineering, Semarang, Indonesia" doesn't specify the institution. The full form should be something like "Department of Computer Science, Faculty of Engineering, Universitas Diponegoro, Semarang, Indonesia."

The email address `ahmad.purnama@university.edu` is a placeholder.

## Table provenance and reproducibility

Table 2 ("Comparison of GNN methods for different seismic tasks") lists performance numbers like "Moment tensor RMSE: 0.12" for van den Ende (2020) and "F1: 0.94 (P), 0.91 (S)" for Bilal et al. (2022). These numbers lack specific citations to tables or figures in the source papers. A reader cannot verify whether these are actual reported numbers, numbers read from a figure, or approximations.

Table 3 (performance comparison) presents ranges like "GNN (Temporal)" achieving "0.02--0.04" P-pick accuracy and "0.96--0.99" detection F1. These ranges aggregate across multiple studies but don't cite which studies contribute each bound. This makes the comparative claims unfalsifiable.

Table 4 (computational requirements) lists specific parameter counts and inference times. The GNN inference times (20--40ms/network for GAT, 50--100ms for Graph Transformer) appear to be estimates rather than measured benchmarks from published work, as no source is cited.

## Section coverage gaps

One structural gap: there is no dedicated "Related Work" or "Previous Reviews" section distinguishing this review from Bergen et al. (2019), Kong et al. (2019), Mousavi & Beroza (2022), and Mousavi (2023). The introduction mentions these but a more explicit positioning would strengthen the novelty claim of "first comprehensive survey dedicated to GNN + EEW."

The Ethical Considerations section (Section 11) runs 3+ pages. For a methods-focused review in Earth-Science Reviews, this may exceed what reviewers expect from a technical review journal. Consider condensing to 1--1.5 pages.

## Speculative claims presented as results

The document sometimes presents speculative capabilities as demonstrated results. The conclusions state "GNN architectures achieve 15--30% reduction in magnitude estimation error" --- this percentage is not anchored to a specific comparison in the body, and the range is so wide as to be unfalsifiable. Similarly, "20--40% improvement in location accuracy" appears without a traceable source. These should either be grounded in specific cited comparisons or explicitly flagged as approximate estimates across the literature.

## BibTeX formatting inconsistencies

- Some arXiv papers use `@article` with `journal={arXiv preprint arXiv:...}` (non-standard for Elsevier)
- `kipf2017semi` is listed as `@article` with `journal={arXiv preprint}` when it was published at ICLR 2017
- `devlin2019bert` cites the arXiv preprint rather than the NAACL 2019 proceedings
- Page ranges use inconsistent dash styles
- `ying2018hierarchical` (DiffPool) is cited but lacks proceedings name

These signal insufficient bibliographic attention and would concern reviewers assessing scholarly rigor.
